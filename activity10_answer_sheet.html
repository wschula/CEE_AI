Stage 1 — Be the model · 10 min · no notebook yet

Twelve real video titles from two Thai YouTube channels: FAROSE (long-form history, biography, travel) and 9arm (tech commentary, gadgets, internet culture). Each card shows four numbers the computer will also see later.

1.A

Guess alone

Pass the laptop around. Each member picks their name, labels all 12 cards without talking, then hands over. Nobody looks at anyone else's picks.

1.B

Agree on rules

Now talk. Write at least three yes/no rules the whole group agrees on, in the order you would check them. A rule names one clue and one outcome, like “If the title contains EP → FAROSE” or “If published before noon → 9arm”.

1.C

Apply the rules and score

Label every card by following your rules only (not your gut). Then press Score: the true channels appear, with each member's score from 1.A and the group's rule-based accuracy. That accuracy goes into the notebook.

1.D

Mine the words

Thai words or phrases you expect to appear more in one channel's titles. At least two per channel, separated by commas. Think about what each channel talks about, not just the 12 cards. In Stage 2, a word that appears in fewer than 5 videos is dropped.

1.E

Describe each channel

One English sentence per channel, at least 8 words, written by the group. Be visual: content, mood, colours, lighting, objects. In Stage 3 this sentence becomes the prompt that repaints a thumbnail, so “tech videos” gives a boring picture and “fast tech commentary, neon studio lighting, smartphones on a desk” gives a good one.

1.F

Hand over to the notebook

This is the baton. Copy the block and paste it over the top part of notebook cell 2.1, replacing the empty values.

Q2.1
Features
A video was published at 18:00 and its title is 45 characters long. Which channel? Which of the five numeric features would you trust least in 2027, and why?

    
Q2.2
Your keywords
Which keyword separated the channels most cleanly? Which one surprised you? Why would you not keep a word that appears in only one or two videos?

    
Q2.3
Three numbers, one trap
The random split usually scores higher. Why is that number a lie about the future? Is the gap between train and test a warning sign, and which of your word features might cause it? Did the machine beat your group's hand-made rules, and did it pick the same top clue?

    
Q2.4
Inside the tree
Translate the first two splits of your tree into plain sentences. Then: why did the rules fail on the video that fooled the tree? Could a human have got it right from the title alone, or would you need to see the video?
